How GSO Village works

A watch-only scale model of downtown Greensboro, North Carolina, lit and peopled by live local data. This page explains where every piece of it comes from, how it is built and published, and how to run it yourself.

What it is

GSO Village is a small isometric model of downtown Greensboro, from Bellemeade Street to Gate City Boulevard, drawn in a browser. It is something you watch, not something you play. The town reacts to what is really happening: it rains on the model when it rains downtown, City Hall lights up on council nights, barrels block a street NCDOT has closed, and a bar's windows glow while it is open.

Beside the model sits "the paper" (The Gate City Almanac), which lists the same things in words: today's events, council meetings, road closures, places to eat and drink, items from elsewhere in the city, and crime counts.

Three rules shape everything:

  • Information shows up as things in the world, not as badges or a dashboard: lit windows, crowds, food trucks, cones.
  • Live information is never invented. A crowd gathers only at a real event and a window lights only where something is really open or on. The everyday cars and walkers are set dressing, and say nothing about real traffic (see Known limits).
  • Private people are not named, and crime never appears on the map (see Privacy rules).

It is a personal, non-commercial community project. The site is static files on GitHub Pages; there is no server, no database and no sign-in. The code is at github.com/ekalb81/gboro.

The collectors write small JSON files into public/data/. The model itself, public/model/downtown.json, is built separately and rarely (see The downtown model). There is no build step for the page: it is plain JavaScript modules and one canvas, with no packages to install.

While the page is open it re-reads the items every minute and the weather and road closures every 5 minutes, so it stays current without a reload. Opening hours and the council state are rechecked against the clock every 30 seconds.

What is on the page

The model

The model fills the window. Drag to look around, scroll or pinch to zoom, and tap a building to read about it. The buttons zoom, return to the Lincoln Financial tower, name the landmarks, and fold the paper away. The camera never moves on its own.

The paper

The paper has sections for Downtown, City Hall, On the roads, Food and drink, Around town and About. Items marked with a pin are on the model: point at one to see where, tap it to go there. Things outside downtown are listed under "Around town" by area, and signposts at the edge of the model point toward each area (UNCG, NC A&T, the Coliseum and so on) with a count of what is on there.

The first citywide map

The project's first version, a hand-placed sketch map of the whole city on a 96 by 60 tile grid, is still served at classic.html. Its layout is public/data/map.json and it is drawn by public/village.js. Some collectors still compute a position on that map as well.

Social posts and events Muse digests in Gmail

Instagram, Facebook and Threads have no free way to read public local posts, so a Meta Muse job does the reading. Every 30 minutes it emails a "Greensboro update" digest to a Gmail inbox. The job collects broadly, anything Greensboro-related, and labels each item with one machine-readable line:

ITEM | category | title | venue | address | start | end | url | account | accountType | posted | verified

Blank fields are written n/a. All filtering happens later in this project's code, not in the Muse prompt.

scripts/fetch-digests.mjs searches Gmail for subject:"Greensboro update" newer_than:14d (override with DIGEST_QUERY), using Gmail's REST API with read-only access, and saves each email's text to data/raw/. It waits and retries when Gmail's per-minute quota is used up. The one-time Google setup (an OAuth client and a refresh token with only the gmail.readonly scope) is written out step by step in the README.

X and TikTok are not covered. A Muse job can also stall while it waits for an approval in Muse, in which case digests stop arriving until it is approved.

Local news and Bluesky RSS and the public Bluesky API

scripts/fetch-feeds.mjs reads the feeds listed in data/sources.json: The Rhino Times, the News & Record, FOX8 and WXII. Regional outlets carry a mustMatch pattern so only Greensboro stories come through. It writes each story as an ITEM line into data/raw/, so news goes through the same ingest, placement and privacy rules as everything else. News from these outlets counts as verified.

Bluesky accounts can be listed in the same file and are read through the public getAuthorFeed endpoint, with no login. Only organizations should be listed. The list is empty today.

Weather and alerts National Weather Service

scripts/fetch-weather.mjs reads the latest observation from the Piedmont Triad airport station (KGSO) and the active alerts for a point downtown, from api.weather.gov. It is free and needs no key. The file keeps the conditions, temperature, wind direction and gusts. The whole model shares that one station's reading.

Road closures NCDOT DriveNC

scripts/fetch-roads.mjs reads NCDOT's traffic events and keeps those inside Greensboro (Guilford County, inside a box around the city, so High Point and the rural county are left out). With DRIVENC_KEY set it uses DriveNC's documented Events API. Without a key it reads the same events from the feeds behind the public DriveNC site, which works but is not a published API and could change.

Each event is a closure, roadwork, a crash or a street closed for an event. There is no free feed of traffic speed or congestion for city streets, so crashes are the only sign of live traffic.

Council meetings and city events the City's schedule, YouTube and news

No official feed of council meetings exists that a script can read, so scripts/civic.mjs combines several sources:

  • The City's published yearly schedule of council and subcommittee meetings is the baseline. Its dates are copied into data/civic-config.json. For a year with no schedule yet, the usual first and third Tuesday rule fills in, labelled as such.
  • Changes during the year are picked up from the City's own posts and verified local news: a cancellation removes a meeting, a special meeting adds one, and a report on the day confirms the start time. Posts by individuals never change the schedule.
  • The City's YouTube feed supplies a "Watch" link for each recorded meeting, cross-checks the schedule (a recording on an unscheduled day adds a meeting, and no recording three days after a scheduled one raises a warning), and supplies the weekly "On the Town" events video.

Disagreements between sources appear as warnings in the publish run's summary. The one yearly chore is copying next year's dates into the config when the City posts them; the run reminds you in December. The full detail is in docs/civic.md.

The City's own website calendar is not used. It sits behind a browser check that scripts cannot pass without defeating it, and it does not list regular council meetings anyway. The City's Legistar feed stopped in June 2021.

Crime counts FBI and City open data

scripts/fetch-crime.mjs has two independent parts:

  • By month, citywide. Violent and property offences for the newest complete month next to the month before, from the FBI Crime Data Explorer for the Greensboro Police Department (agency code NC0410200). It needs a free api.data.gov key in FBI_API_KEY, and runs about six weeks behind.
  • By week, per police division. From the City's open data layer "GPD - Crime", with no key. The City's feed stopped on October 6, 2025, so this table is hidden for now and comes back on its own when the feed is current again.

Only totals are ever requested or stored. The City fetcher asks for four fields (incident id, division, offence code, report date) and never for addresses, coordinates or anything about victims; a test checks this.

Restaurants and bars OpenStreetMap

scripts/fetch-eats.mjs asks OpenStreetMap for named restaurants, bars, pubs, nightclubs, cafes, takeout and breweries around Greensboro. Chains (anything with a brand tag) are left out. It tries the Overpass API first and, because Overpass is often busy, falls back to about 40 small requests to the main OpenStreetMap API. Places change slowly, so the publish run only refetches when its copy is more than 20 hours old.

Opening hours come from OSM's opening_hours tag for the common shapes. Where hours are missing or too unusual to parse, the site uses typical hours for that kind of place and says it is a guess.

Privacy rules

The Muse digest collects broadly, so the rules for a public site live in code, in applyRules in scripts/lib.mjs, and are covered by tests.

  • Individuals are never named. An item from a private person's account is shown without the account name.
  • Personal posts from individuals are held back (the "community" and "other" categories).
  • Unverified news is held back.
  • Each item shows a short summary and a link to the original public post, not a copy of it.
  • Crime is totals only, in the paper only, never drawn on the model.

Raw digests and held items name private people, so they never reach git or the site. data/raw/, data/held.json and public/data/items.json are git-ignored. Each publish run starts from a clean checkout and refetches, raw text lives only for the length of that run, and the run refuses to deploy if a held file or any raw text file is found in public/. Raw data is deliberately not kept in an Actions cache either, because pull requests from forks of a public repository can read caches.

The site sets no tracking cookies and collects nothing about visitors. The full policy, including the Google API terms, is in PRIVACY.md. To ask for an item to be removed, open an issue.

The downtown model

The model covers the box from 36.0768 to 36.0676 north and 79.7954 to 79.7856 west, at 9 metres per tile (about 98 by 113 tiles). The file records that box, so anything with a latitude and longitude lands in the right place. It holds 272 buildings, 438 street segments, 77 signals and stop signs, 633 trees and about 7,200 parking stalls.

Rebuilding the model

Three Python tools in tools/model/ build the model files. They are run by hand when the inputs change; the publish run does not rebuild the model.

ToolWritesWhen to run it
ground.pydata/model/imagery/ground.json: ground cover in 1.5 m cells and the direction of painted parking linesOnly after a new statewide aerial flight
trees.py lidardata/model/osm/trees-lidar.json: tree crowns from the lidarOnly after a new lidar survey
trees.pypublic/model/trees.jsonWith each model rebuild
prep.pypublic/model/downtown.jsonWhen OpenStreetMap or a survey layer changes. Takes about 3 minutes.
py tools\model\prep.py downtown-osm.json path\to\survey-inputs public\model\downtown.json

The tools need Python 3 with numpy, pillow, scipy and shapely. The large survey inputs (the aerial image, lidar-derived heights and shapes, roof and facade colours, the OpenStreetMap extract) are kept in the project's working files, not in git; only the two small derived files under data/model/ are committed. Any input that is missing is skipped. A newer downtown.json drops in with no code change.

The renderer

public/model.js draws everything on plain 2D canvases, with no libraries.

  • Tiles. The still parts of the model are drawn once into 512-pixel tiles at the current zoom, only where the camera looks, and kept until the light or the scene changes. Tiles just outside the view and at the next zoom levels are drawn ahead in spare frame time, so drags and zooms rarely wait.
  • Draw order. Buildings are ordered back to front by line of sight, not by their centre points, so long and L-shaped buildings sort correctly.
  • What hides what. Anything partly behind a building, tree, lamp or parked car is drawn on a small scratch card, the outlines in front are cut out of it, and the rest is laid onto the picture. Nothing in front is ever repainted.
  • Light. Shadows are cast from the real position of the sun. The colour grade, lit windows, glow and a tilt-shift blur are laid on top each frame.

Trees, seasons and wind

public/trees.js gives each tree one of 15 species with a Greensboro calendar for bloom, leaf-out, fall colour and leaf drop. No public inventory of downtown's trees exists, so the species are inferred from what is known about each tree: evergreen or not, height, crown width, and whether it stands on a street or in a park. The positions and sizes are measured; the species are educated guesses.

Cars, people and the train

The moving cars, walkers and train are a simulation, there to make the town feel alive. Cars drive the real street network (one lane each way, one-way streets respected), follow the car ahead, stop at the real signals and stop signs from OpenStreetMap and yield on side streets. Signal timings are typical, not the real ones. Parked cars sit in the real stalls of each lot, about two thirds full. Only the road closures are live data.

Performance

npm run bench opens the model in a headless browser across desktop, laptop and phone scenarios, including dragging and zooming, and prints frame times by phase. How to run it, how to read it and what has been done so far are in docs/PERFORMANCE.md.

Publishing

The site is live at ekalb81.github.io/gboro, on GitHub Pages. One workflow, .github/workflows/publish.yml, does everything. It runs every 30 minutes (at 7 and 37 past the hour), on every push to main, and on demand from the Actions tab.

  1. Run the tests (npm test).
  2. Restore the last good copies of the weather, crime counts, restaurants and bars, and the City's YouTube feed from the Actions cache. These hold only public or aggregate data.
  3. Run every collector, then ingest (node scripts/refresh.mjs). A collector whose script or key is missing is skipped, not failed. Only an ingest failure fails the run.
  4. Save those four files back to the cache.
  5. Check that the site has its files and that no private file is in public/.
  6. Deploy public/ to the host named in the DEPLOY_TARGET variable.

Because every run starts clean and refetches, items from a source that is down for a run drop off the site until the next good run. The weather, crime, restaurants and council video keep their last copy instead.

Cloudflare Pages is wired up as an alternative host. Switching hosts, taking the site down and the reasons behind these choices are in PUBLISHING.md.

GitHub often starts scheduled runs 5 to 15 minutes late, and pauses the schedule on a public repository after 60 days without a commit. If the site stops updating, re-enable the workflow under Actions.

Running it locally

You need Node.js 20 or newer and git. There are no packages to install. The commands below are for Windows Command Prompt.

git clone https://github.com/ekalb81/gboro.git
cd gboro
npm test
npm run ingest
npm run weather
npm run roads
npm run serve

Then open http://localhost:8080. With no keys you get the model, the weather and the road closures; the paper's other sections stay empty until their collectors have run.

npm run dev chains those steps in one command, but it is written for a Unix shell. In Command Prompt it stops if the weather or roads fetch fails, so the separate commands above are the dependable way on Windows.

Running the collectors with keys

Create a file named .env in the repo folder (it is git-ignored) with one NAME=value line per secret from the table above, then:

node --env-file=.env scripts\refresh.mjs
npm run serve

That runs every collector and the ingest, exactly as the publish run does. Useful variations:

CommandDoes
node scripts\refresh.mjs --listShows which collectors are ready and which are missing a key
node scripts\refresh.mjs --only=weatherRuns one collector, then ingest
npm run fetch -- --dry-runLists the digests it would save, changing nothing
npm run feeds -- --days=3News from the last 3 days only
npm run civic, npm run crime, npm run eatsOne collector on its own
node scripts\serve.mjs 9000Serves on another port
npm run benchThe performance benchmark (needs Playwright; see docs/PERFORMANCE.md)

To set a single key for one Command Prompt window without a file, use set DRIVENC_KEY=your-key and then run the command.

Adding a data source

  1. Write the collector as scripts/fetch-<source>.mjs. It either writes its own JSON file into public/data/, as the weather does, or writes ITEM lines into data/raw/ so ingest and the privacy rules handle it, as the news feeds do. It should exit with an error on failure and leave its last good output in place.
  2. Add one entry to scripts/collectors.json with its name and script, and optionally args, env (the keys it needs) and timeoutMinutes (default 5).
  3. If it needs a key, add the secret in the repository settings and pass it in the "Collect data and ingest" step of publish.yml.
  4. Teach the page to show it: load the file in public/app.js, add it to the scene handed to the model, and write its lines into the paper.
  5. Add tests under test/, with a saved sample of the source's response as a fixture.

A source must be dependable and automatic. Anything that needs a file downloaded by hand each week, or a script running on someone's own computer, does not qualify.

Known limits

  • Downtown only on the model. The rest of the city appears in the paper under "Around town" and on the edge signposts.
  • Traffic is simulated. The moving cars and walkers do not reflect real traffic or real crowds. Only closures, roadwork and crashes are live, and signal timings are typical.
  • Building heights are from 2015. A handful of buildings finished since then, such as the Tanger Center and Hyatt Place, have estimated heights. The county was flown again in 2024, and those heights have not been brought in yet.
  • The model is rebuilt by hand. New buildings and street changes in OpenStreetMap appear only after a rebuild.
  • Tree species are inferred, as described above.
  • One weather station. The reading is from the airport, about nine miles west of downtown.
  • Crime is coarse and late. Citywide monthly totals run about six weeks behind, and the per-division weekly table is waiting on the City's feed, which stopped in October 2025.
  • Social coverage depends on the Muse job. If it stalls, events stop arriving, and events older than 14 days of digests drop off. X and TikTok are not covered.
  • Some news feeds refuse GitHub's servers at times. The News & Record feed has answered "too many requests" to the publish run. A feed that fails is skipped for that run.
  • Individuals' post links. An individual's account name is hidden, but the link to their public post can still reveal who posted it.
  • Opening hours can be wrong or guessed where OpenStreetMap has none. Guesses are labelled.
  • The council schedule needs one update a year, when the City posts the next year's dates.
  • The keyless DriveNC route is unofficial and could break; the keyed API is the one in use on the live site.
  • Performance varies by browser. Benchmarks so far are in Chromium. The model has been seen to run slowly in Safari on high-resolution screens, and that work is ongoing.
  • Up to 30 minutes behind. Data is as fresh as the last publish run, which GitHub may start late.
